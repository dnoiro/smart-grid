## Interview Questions You Should Be Ready For

### Q: "Tell me about your power system modeling experience."
**A**: *"I have hands-on experience developing distribution network models for steady-state analysis. In my smart grid project, I worked with a multi-bus system analyzing voltage and current phasor measurements to perform power flow calculations and state estimation. This directly applies to BESS and microgrid modeling where you need to understand power flows, voltage profiles, and the impact of distributed resources on the distribution network."*

### Q: "How does this relate to BESS or microgrid work?"
**A**: *"The analytical framework I developed is directly applicable to BESS and microgrid projects. For BESS siting, you need to analyze potential integration points in the distribution network—exactly what I did with my network topology optimization. You need to understand voltage and current impacts, which I analyzed using phasor measurements. For microgrids, you need to evaluate different network configurations and understand power flows under various conditions—I analyzed three different topologies and multiple load scenarios. The optimization algorithms I implemented could be adapted for optimal BESS placement or microgrid boundary definition."*

### Q: "What specific tools or software did you use?"
**A**: *"I implemented the analysis in Python, leveraging pandas for CSV data processing and time-series analysis, along with custom algorithms for the optimization framework. I developed Particle Swarm Optimization algorithms from scratch and worked with structured measurement data containing voltage and current phasors. While this was a custom implementation, the underlying principles are the same as commercial power system software like PSS/E or PowerWorld. I'm comfortable learning new tools quickly and understand the fundamental power system concepts that transcend any particular software package."*

### Q: "Have you worked with real-world utility data or systems?"
**A**: *"This project used simulated distribution network data with realistic voltage and current measurements structured like SCADA system outputs. While it was an academic exercise, I designed the framework to be applicable to real-world scenarios—hence the multiple operating conditions, different network configurations, and realistic bus/line representation. I understand the difference between academic projects and production utility systems, and I'm eager to gain hands-on experience with actual utility infrastructure and real-time operational data at Duke Energy."*
